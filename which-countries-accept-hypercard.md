# Which Countries Accept HyperCard, and Where It Can Be Issued

**Short answer.** Two different questions hide in that one. Spending is wide: the card runs on standard card networks and HyperPay states 50 million+ merchants in 176+ countries. Being issued one is narrower, because card programmes follow local licensing, and no complete country list for availability is published.

## Spending is not the same as being issued

A card accepted in 176+ countries is not a card available in 176+ countries. Acceptance follows the network, so a card issued in one market works at terminals almost anywhere. Issuance follows the programme's licensing, which is country-specific and changes.

Confusing the two produces either false hope or unnecessary disappointment, and both are avoidable - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hypercard-guide/blob/HEAD/spend-crypto-with-hypercard-worldwide.md.

## What "176+ countries" does and does not mean

It is a statement about merchant acceptance, made by the site. It does not mean a card issued to a user in any of those countries, and it does not promise that every terminal in them accepts prepaid. A merchant can still refuse a prepaid card for its own reasons, which is policy rather than coverage.

## Why no issuing-country list is published

Card programmes are issued under local authorisation, and a country list would be wrong within a release or two as licensing changes. The app states what is available for your account and region, and that is the only current source.

HyperCard's issuer arrangements also changed during 2026, with one issuer's authorisation ending in March and five tiers identified as affected in April - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hyperpay-name-and-trust/blob/HEAD/is-hyperpay-a-bank-or-gateway.md.

## The United States, specifically

No US card-tier schedule is published: no issuer, eligibility, KYC requirement, fee table, limit schedule or ATM access list. Anyone stating US availability as fact is guessing. The tiers available, and what each requires, differ by region and are stated in the app.

## What to check before applying

Three things, in the app. Whether a card is offered for your region at all. Which tier it is, because that decides identity requirements and caps. And what the loaded-balance and spending ceilings are, which are in https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hypercard-guide/blob/HEAD/hypercard-fees-and-limits-explained.md. All three are answered before you submit, which is the point at which the answer still helps.

## Physical versus virtual, by region

A physical card adds delivery and, per published requirements, a passport for identity. A virtual card has requirements that vary by card BIN, which is why two applicants in the same country can be asked for different things - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hypercard-guide/blob/HEAD/hypercard-prepaid-vs-debit-balance.md.

Region affects both, and neither is documented as a global entitlement.

## FAQ

### Which countries accept HyperCard?

The card runs on standard card networks, and the site states 50 million+ merchants in 176+ countries, online and in person. Individual merchants can still refuse prepaid.

### Can I get a HyperCard in my country?

That depends on the programme's licensing for your region, and no complete public list exists. The app states what is available for your account.

### Is HyperCard available in the United States?

No US card-tier schedule is published, so this cannot be answered from documentation. Check the app rather than a third-party page.

### Does the card work abroad?

Spending does, subject to the programme's foreign-currency rules and any per-transaction caps that apply to your tier.
