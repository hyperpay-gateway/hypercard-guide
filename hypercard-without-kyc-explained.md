# HyperCard Without KYC: What the No-KYC Tier Actually Means

**Short answer.** A no-KYC tier does exist for HyperCard, and it is not a KYC-free card for everyone: identity thresholds and spending limits vary by tier and region, and the app states what applies before you apply. Whether it is available in the United States is not published anywhere, so the only reliable answer comes from the app for your account.

## The tier exists, the terms are not global

HyperPay states that the prepaid card has a tier requiring no traditional KYC, alongside tiers that do. What the site does not publish is a country-by-country matrix of which tier is open where. That gap is deliberate: a tier open in one market can be closed in another.

That gap is deliberate rather than evasive: card programmes are issued under local licensing, and a tier available in one market can be closed in another. A published list would be wrong within a release or two.

## What "no KYC" cannot mean

Three things are worth separating, because marketing copy blurs them. No traditional identity verification at sign-up. No bank account or credit check, which is true of every HyperCard tier. And no restrictions whatsoever, which is not true of any regulated card.

HyperPay's own framing is the second one: no bank account, no credit check, no local banking relationship - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hypercard-guide/blob/HEAD/what-is-hypercard-prepaid-card.md. That is a real advantage and it is not the same as an unverified card.

## Limits are the trade

A lower-verification tier carries lower limits. That is how every card programme works, and it is the honest way to read "no KYC": you get a card without a document trail, and you get a smaller ceiling on what it can move.

The ceilings, per tier and region, are shown in the app before applying - https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hypercard-guide/blob/HEAD/hypercard-spending-limits-by-tier.md.

## Is it available in the United States?

Not published. There is no US card-tier schedule - issuer, eligibility, KYC requirement, fees, limits or ATM access - anywhere in the documentation this page works from, and the card's issuer arrangements changed in 2026 in ways that are only partly public (https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hyperpay-name-and-trust/blob/HEAD/is-hyperpay-a-bank-or-gateway.md).

So the answer is not "yes" or "no". It is: check the app for your account and region, and treat any page that promises US availability as unsourced.

## What the application asks for

Every tier asks for something. Even a no-KYC tier needs an account and, typically, a phone or email verification, because the card has to be issued to someone and topped up from a wallet balance. Where documents are required, a passport is the one named for a physical card.

What differs is whether identity documents are part of it. Where they are - typically for physical cards - a passport is the document named, and the application route is the same one every tier uses: https://github.com/hyperpay-gateway/hypercard-guide/blob/HEAD/apply-for-a-hypercard-crypto-card.md.

## Reading the tier table without being misled

Ask three questions of any tier comparison, including this product's. What does verification require? What limits come with it? And which jurisdictions is it open in? A tier described without those three answers is a slogan, and the app is the only current source.

A tier described without those three is a slogan. The app answers all three for your account before you apply, which is the only place the answer is current. ## FAQ

### Is there a no-KYC option for HyperCard?

Yes, a no-KYC tier exists. Identity thresholds and spending limits vary by tier and region, and the app states what applies before you apply.

### Is the no-KYC tier available in the United States?

Not published. No US card-tier schedule exists in the documentation available, so the app is the only current source for your region.

### Does no-KYC mean no verification at all?

No. It means no traditional identity documents for that tier; an account and contact verification still apply, and limits are lower.

### Are the limits lower without KYC?

Usually, yes - that is the trade in every card programme, and the specific ceilings are shown in the app.
